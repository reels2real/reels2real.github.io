People searching for "sale" are the most purchase-intent visitors on any site — they have made a decision to buy, they're just looking for the right price. A 51% bounce means the page is failing them at the moment of highest intent.

Likely causes: page loads slowly on mobile, filters not surfacing well-discounted items first, no urgency element (countdown, stock indicator), or the discount depth isn't compelling enough above the fold.

Actions
This Week
Test the sale page on mobile — time the load, check if filters work, check what's visible above the fold without scrolling
Most sale traffic is mobile. If this page is slow or broken on mobile, that's the full explanation.
This Week
Sort sale products by "highest discount %" by default — show the best deals first
Most WooCommerce sale pages default to "latest" which buries the best discounts.
Week 2
Add a clear banner above the product grid: "Up to 40% off — ends [date]" with a countdown timer
Urgency reduces bounce rate. Even a soft deadline ("while stocks last") improves conversion.
Week 2

People leave when they can't find what they want. Better filters = longer session = more likely purchase.

Google organic (21,580 sessions) is the strongest real channel and it's free. This validates investing in SEO — the site already ranks well, it just needs more content and better product page optimisation.

Meta paid (~18,884 sessions) is active but the campaign structure is fragmented — 30+ different campaign names and ad sets in the data, many with 1–3 sessions. Consolidating into fewer, better-structured campaigns will improve performance at the same spend level.

Google CPC (7,796 sessions) is described as "not cost-effective." The issue is likely the product feed quality — Google Shopping needs clean titles, proper categorisation and competitive pricing signals. The traffic is available; the feed isn't optimised to capture it.

Instagram social (1,466) — organic Instagram is generating very little traffic. The content isn't driving clicks to the site. Reels with product links and bio link optimisation (Linktree shows 47 sessions — confirm it's updated) can improve this significantly.

Internal Search — A Map of What Customers Actually Want
People who use site search have the highest purchase intent. Their queries reveal gaps and opportunities.
Search data reveals 4 clear product discovery problems
Co-ord sets, Plus size, Offline reorders, and specific product categories are all being searched but not easily found
Product Intelligence

Most searched categories — validated demand, confirm these have strong landing pages:

Problem 1 — Co-ord sets are searched in 15+ different spellings

Co-ord setCo ord setCoord setCord setCoard setCo-od setCoordsetCord setsCoodset

15+ spelling variants for the same product means people cannot find it through navigation. They're typing because the menu path isn't clear. A dedicated "Co-ord Sets" category page with a clean URL and prominent navigation placement would capture all of this demand without needing search.

Problem 2 — Plus size audience is searching but not landing
"Plus size", "Plus size tops", "Plus size kurtis", "Plus size clothing", "6XL", "7XL", "Jeans 7XL", "5XL gowns" all appear in search. Mustard's XS–6XL sizing is a genuine competitive advantage that isn't being communicated clearly enough — this audience is already on the site but can't find the right products.

Problem 3 — Offline customers searching by SKU code to reorder
Searches like A2507, T1206, D1194, A2531, F1226, T1233 are store customers who bought a product offline and are trying to reorder it online. This is the offline-to-online bridge happening organically — and there's no system to capture it. These are the highest-intent visitors on the site.

Problem 4 — Seasonal gaps: "Winter", "Festive", "Party wear" searched but likely hard to find
"Winter kurti", "Festive wear", "Party wear kurtis", "Summer dresses" — these are occasion-based searches that suggest customers think in terms of events, not product types. Category pages organised by occasion (not just product type) would serve these searchers better.

Actions
This Week
Add "Co-ord Sets" as a top-level navigation item with a clean category page — not buried under a sub-menu
Also add search synonyms in WooCommerce so all spellings resolve to the same page.
This Week
Create a "Plus Size" category/filter page highlighting the XS–6XL range — this is a competitive differentiator that isn't visible enough
A dedicated plus size landing page also ranks well on Google for "plus size ethnic wear online India".
Week 2
Add a "Find by style code" search helper and ensure all products have their offline SKU code in the product description and searchable metadata
Customers who bought in-store and come online to reorder are the easiest conversion possible. Don't make them search and fail.
