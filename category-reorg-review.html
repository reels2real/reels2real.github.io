Category reorganisation — current state, proposal, and the decisions still open

A review document. It sets out how the catalogue is organised on the live WooCommerce site today, what the proposed nine-category structure looks like, how every existing menu item maps into it, and which questions have to be answered before any of it is built. All figures were read directly from the live site on 31 July 2026.

Contents

  2. The menu today, and the menu proposed
  3. Mapping — every menu item, and where it goes
  4. Coverage — do all products still have a home?
  5. The seven decisions (D1–D7), in full

The short version. The proposal is a smaller change than it first appears. WooCommerce already runs a seven-item menu that includes New Arrivals and Kurta Set at top level, so moving to nine is two additions — the substantive changes are that Top wear dissolves into four separate categories and Sale loses a level.

Of the seven open questions, two are already answered by the data (D2 and D4 — both turned out to be products that were never migrated, not products that could not be classified), and only three genuinely need the shop owner: D3, D6 and D7. The one real blocker is D1, which is a strategic choice rather than a data question.

Section four

Coverage — do all products still have a home?

Every one of the 1,559 published products was tested against the proposed structure. 75 land nowhere as it is currently specified. None of that is caused by the reorganisation — most of it already exists — but the reorganisation is the moment to fix it.

The other 30 homeless products are not a structural problem — they were simply never migrated. 18 sit only in Sale categories, and 6 are Co-Ord Sets and 4 camisoles filed under Top wear alone. Together with the 15 Kurta Sets, they are part of the 50 published products that have never been copied across, because the last synchronisation ran on 29 June 2026. Around 20 of those 50 are ordinary new stock that maps perfectly well. Run the migration again before judging any category's size.

Section five

The seven decisions, in full

These are referenced as D1–D7 throughout the tables above. Each names who has to answer it. Filled circles mark the recommendation.

D1

Does the nine-category structure go ahead?

Owner + developer · blocker

Everything else depends on this one. The nine-way structure flattens Festive and Winterwear into single categories, which removes eleven working links from the menu, and it dissolves Top wear into four separate items. Both are deliberate simplifications, but they are choices rather than fixes.

  • Go ahead as specified — nine items, two levels throughout.
  • Go ahead but keep Festive and Winterwear's dropdowns, so no links are lost. This is a middle path and costs nothing structurally.
  • Keep the current seven-item shape and apply only the data fixes in D2–D7.
⚠️ Worth knowing: the Shopify store currently runs a different four-column menu again, modelled on a demo theme. Whichever option is chosen, that will be rebuilt to match — it is not a third alternative.
D2

What counts as a Kurta Set?

Answered — no input needed

This was open for months on the belief that nothing in the catalogue identified a two-piece set. That was wrong. WooCommerce has a top-level Kurta Set category holding 15 products, and it is the second item in the live menu. Each is named Kurta: T1247 / Bottom: F1247 …, carrying both garment codes in the title.

All 15 sit in that category and no other, which is exactly why they were never copied to Shopify: the migration assigns categories first, and a product whose only category is unrecognised gets skipped. They are missing, not unclassifiable.

  • Map the category and re-run the migration for those 15 products.
  • Also bring across the 13 individually-sold halves of these sets, which are currently filed only under Sale.
✓ The Kurta: … / Bottom: … naming is a reliable marker, and the co-ord sets follow the same convention. Worth keeping as the rule if more two-piece stock arrives.
D3

Are Festive and Winterwear separate wardrobes, or views of the same one?

Owner

The Festive Palazzos (37) and the Winter Palazzo (21) share no products with the main Palazzo category (82) — they are three separate sets of garments, not three views of one. So a shopper browsing Bottom wear never sees 58 palazzos that exist on the site. The same applies to Slacks, Dhotis, Leggings and Skirts, which hold 14 products between them that sit in no other category.

  • Fold them all into Bottom Wear, and keep the Festive and Winterwear links as alternative routes to the same garments. Nothing is lost and Bottom wear stops under-counting.
  • Keep Festive and Winterwear as deliberately separate wardrobes, and accept that Bottom wear shows only part of the range.
⚠️ If nothing is done, 14 garments have no place in the new structure at all — 6 leggings, 5 dhotis, 2 slacks and 1 skirt.
D4

Why does Co-ord sets look almost empty?

Answered — one optional question

Because it was being measured against the wrong thing. The menu item Co-Ord Sets points at a product tag, not a category — the tag holds 26 products while the co-ords category holds 1. Anything that maps co-ord sets by category will find almost nothing.

Measured correctly, the data is sound: 26 tagged in WooCommerce, 20 copied across, and the missing 6 are recent stock added after the last synchronisation. The related Ensembles (18) and Wedding Collection (13) collections are exact carryovers with nothing missing.

  • Re-run the migration to collect the 6. No judgement required.
  • Optional, owner's call: around 50 products have "co-ord" in their name but were never tagged. Widen the tag to include them, or leave the tag as deliberate curation?
✓ Broader lesson: WooCommerce carries merchandising in tags as well as categories, and two menu items are tag-driven. Any mapping built only from the category list will miss them.
D5

Where do dupattas belong?

Owner

Dupattas are currently filed under Top wear (50) and again under Festive (26). The proposal moves them under Kurta & Kurta Sets. This matters more than it sounds: dupattas are the single largest group inside Top wear that is not a top, and leaving them there is what made them flood the "new arrivals" listings earlier this year.

  • Put them under Kurta & Kurta Sets, and keep the Festive link as a second route.
  • Give Dupattas their own top-level item — they are an accessory, not a garment.
  • Leave them in Top wear.
D6

What happens to empty and unused categories?

Owner · housekeeping

Five categories hold no products at all (Shrugs, T-Shirts, Capris, Festive Dhotis, and the empty Sale winter branch). A further group holds products but appears in no menu. Separately, 33 products sit in "Uncategorized" and need someone to say what they are — no script can decide that.

  • Delete the empty ones; keep the populated but unlinked ones reachable by URL, which preserves search rankings.
  • Delete nothing — no risk, but the admin stays cluttered.
  • Delete everything unlinked — ⚠️ this breaks live URLs that may be linked from elsewhere.
⚠️ The 33 Uncategorized products are the only genuinely unanswerable item in this document. Everything else can be resolved from data.
D7

Does the old New Arrivals listing retire?

Owner · housekeeping

There is an older New Arrivals collection of 128 products on Shopify that has been superseded by a newer arrangement. It is not linked from anywhere, but its address may still be shared or indexed.

  • Point it at the new New Arrivals page with a redirect, so old links keep working.
  • Leave it in place, unlinked.
  • Delete it.
